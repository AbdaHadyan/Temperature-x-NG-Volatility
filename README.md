## Abstract

This study investigates whether the three-day-ahead temperature forecast errors are associated with subsequent realised variance in Dutch TTF natural gas futures, and whether this association varies across the conditional distribution of variance. Using data from 2022 to 2025, a Heterogeneous Autoregressive (HAR-RV) specification is estimated via ordinary least squares to assess the conditional mean relationship, controlling for volatility persistence, gas storage levels, heating demand, geopolitical risk, and seasonality. The analysis is extended using quantile regression to examine whether the relationship differs across the conditional distribution. The OLS results indicate that temperature forecast errors exhibit only a weak, statistically insignificant association with the conditional mean of subsequent realised variance. 
However, quantile regression reveals a positive and statistically significant association at the upper quantiles, particularly at the 90th percentile, suggesting that the relationship between unexpected temperature information and natural gas variance is concentrated in the upper tail of the distribution. This pattern is broadly consistent across both the full sample and a post-crisis subsample excluding the 2022 European energy crisis, providing preliminary evidence that the result is not solely driven by the exceptional conditions of the 2022 European energy crisis. The findings suggest that the relationship between meteorological forecast uncertainty and natural gas market risk is primarily a tail phenomenon that may be obscured by conditional-mean models.
